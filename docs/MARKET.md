# AgentCourt AI — Market Thesis

## The concern

The current number of disputes directly involving autonomous AI agents is still limited.

That is a real market risk.

AgentCourt AI should therefore avoid depending exclusively on "AI-agent disputes" as the immediate customer category.

## Near-term market

The initial market can be broader:

- decentralized arbitration
- DAOs
- Web3 marketplaces
- protocol operations
- escrow workflows
- contributor/freelancer disputes
- crypto commerce

These markets already produce evidence-heavy disputes.

## 2–5 year thesis

As autonomous agents become capable of:

- holding assets
- purchasing services
- negotiating
- hiring other agents
- executing contracts
- operating continuously

the volume and complexity of machine-originated disputes should increase.

AgentCourt AI's strategy is to establish the evidence infrastructure before that demand becomes obvious.

## Risk

This is a timing bet.

If agent-to-agent commerce develops slower than expected, AgentCourt still needs a viable business in today's evidence-heavy Web3 dispute market.

## Positioning

Near term:

**Evidence intelligence for decentralized dispute workflows.**

Long term:

**Trust and evidence infrastructure for the autonomous economy.**

This reduces market-timing risk while preserving the larger vision.

## Investor metrics

The project should not rely on vanity metrics.

Track:

- active organizations
- active reviewers
- cases processed
- evidence items processed
- average review time
- time saved per case
- repeat case rate
- API usage
- partner-sourced cases
- revenue
- gross margin
- reviewer retention
- dispute-cycle reduction

Until these exist, traction should be described as not yet established.
